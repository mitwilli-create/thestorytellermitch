01 / What Deepgram launched

The product is strong. The story is waiting.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

What the launch established

Deepgram shipped Flux as a conversation-native model for real-time voice agents, not just a prettier text-to-speech voice. Scott’s interview added the operating proof: more than 2,000 products, more than 200,000 developers, more than $100 million in annual recurring revenue, and response times as low as 80 milliseconds.

What the launch left open

Scott also explained the architecture behind the experience: fast voice interaction beside slower reasoning and customer data, with API, virtual private cloud, Bedrock, and adaptive model options. The editorial question remains: what does recovery feel like when a caller corrects the agent?

02 / What I would change

Lead with the human moment. Then make it travel.

The launch had the product and the proof points. I would change the editorial order, the audience handoff, and the reader action.

What I would change

What Deepgram shipped.

What I would have made.

The launch led with product and model details.

I would lead with the interruption, prove it with audio, and route each audience to a next action.

They shipped
  • Technical explanation.
  • Founder point of view.
  • Developer invitation to try Flux.
I would add
  • A human opening question.
  • A controlled before-and-after audio proof.
  • Separate buyer, builder, and press routes.

03 / Route itGive each audience a next action.

Evaluate, run the test, or cover the consequence.

One-week deliverable

A 60-second interruption test.

Record the same exchange twice: Flux recovers after the correction; a baseline repeats the wrong number. Publish the audio, transcript, and test fixture.

Choose a beat to hear the proof.

Output: matched audio, a transcript, and a public test. Measure full listens, test starts, and evaluation requests.

What this makes tangible: the trust problem behind interruption handling, in a form a buyer can hear and a builder can repeat.

03 / What I would do over 90 days

Turn one launch into a conversation.

Start with the Flux recovery story. Use events, product releases, customer access, and online discussion to decide where Deepgram can add evidence instead of noise.

Week 1 / Find the opening

Record and report the interruption test.

Interview a Flux product expert, review the launch and current voice-agent discussions, write the question “Can the agent recover after a correction?”, then record the same script twice: one clean recovery and one repeated response.

DeliverA reviewed 60-second audio comparison, story brief, and public test.MeasureAudio starts, full listens, test starts, and evaluation requests.StopIf the method or result cannot be disclosed, keep the package internal.
Days 8 to 30 / Publish and learn

Publish the lead package.

Publish the reported feature, matched audio, short video, transcript, and builder test. Use events, product releases, and current voice-agent discussion to choose the right distribution across LinkedIn, newsletters, documentation, the playground, Hacker News, Reddit, X, GitHub, podcasts, and press.

DeliverOne package with the same facts and a clear next action for each audience.MeasureAudio completions, evaluation requests, documentation visits, playground starts, and completed tests.StopRevise a format that earns attention but no usable audience action.
Days 31 to 60 / Earn enterprise proof

Report a real deployment decision.

Interview a named customer, operator, or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow, what changed, and what happened next. If access is unavailable, use public evidence about reliability, deployment, privacy, or operational risk without claiming a customer story.

DeliverEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer brief, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise requests, target-account conversations, and completed buyer paths.StopKeep the customer story open if there is no named source, permission, decision, and result.
Days 61 to 90 / Decide what holds

Repeat, revise, stop, or codify the method.

Repeat the package if it produces audience action and usable evidence. Revise the format if one moves without the other. Stop if neither moves. Codify the test only if Deepgram can publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, code, and weak results, then rerun it consistently across tone, timing, interruption handling, emotional context, and consistency.
