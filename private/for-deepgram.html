What I would have made from Flux.

The launch had a human story, a technical proof point, and a live audience window. This is how I would have turned those into an editorial package for enterprise buyers, builders, press, and the communities already discussing voice.

01 · Explain the product first

What Flux is

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for voice agents. It turns written responses into spoken language quickly and is designed for live conversations where people interrupt, change their minds, and expect the voice to remain consistent.

The editorial question

Can a voice agent handle the half second after a caller cuts it off, or does the conversation fall apart?

01 · ExplainWhat it does

Flux creates spoken responses for real-time voice agents.

02 · ShowPlay the proof

Use one interruption in an old and new system.

03 · ChoosePick a path

Let visitors choose business evaluation or building.

04 · ActMake it easy

Offer an enterprise conversation or a developer test.

02 · Editorial judgment

What Deepgram produced. What I would have produced.

The product shipped. The weakness was the editorial shape. The launch combined technical and business audiences, then left the strongest human story behind.

Deepgram produced
  • Led across the launch surfaces with model details, company scale, revenue, and availability.
  • Opened the article with a strong human scene, then moved away from it.
  • Named interruptions in Scott’s LinkedIn post, but did not build the launch package around them.
  • Used a founder interview whose strongest explanation arrived late.
  • Served enterprise buyers and developers without a clear audience tiebreaker.
I would have produced
  • Explained Flux in plain language before separating the business and developer paths.
  • Led with the interruption story and used audio to prove it.
  • Moved Scott’s strongest explanation into the opening argument.
  • Added customer, partner, engineer, podcast, video, and developer treatments.
  • Set a different action and success measure for each audience.

03 · Three immediate stories

Three stories. Three full packages.

Each story has one editorial centerpiece and a set of supporting formats. The formats serve the story. They do not become a list of marketing assets.

01 · Lead story

The half second after you cut off a voice agent

A reported story about interruption, memory, and the moment a caller decides whether to trust the system.

Audience
Enterprise decision makers first, then builders and press.
Centerpiece
Deepgram Learn article with a before and after audio demonstration.
Package
Audio clip, 60 to 90 second video, Scott LinkedIn essay, engineer explanation, podcast segment, and developer fixture.
Distribution
Enterprise press, LinkedIn, voice podcasts, Hacker News, Reddit discussion, X, GitHub, and documentation.
Success
Qualified enterprise interest, relevant discussion, audio plays, developer tests, and useful press conversations.

Dealbreaker: no unsupported customer story or unfair comparison test.

02 · Enterprise proof

What enterprises need before they trust a voice agent

Use IBM’s public statement as independent enterprise proof, without presenting IBM as a Flux customer unless that is confirmed.

Audience
Enterprise buyers, business press, and partner teams.
Centerpiece
Reported enterprise story about reliability, deployment, privacy, and operational trust.
Package
IBM or partner interview, executive LinkedIn version, short video, enterprise podcast treatment, and sales enablement summary.
Distribution
Deepgram Learn, IBM and Deepgram channels if approved, enterprise newsletters, and targeted business press.
Success
Enterprise conversations, partner engagement, and use in qualified sales discussions.

Dealbreaker: no implication that IBM approved claims it has not approved.

03 · Customer story

Can a voice agent take an order over engine noise?

A reported drive-through story about noise, interruptions, food names, prices, order changes, and human handoffs.

Audience
Restaurant operators, enterprise buyers, customer experience leaders, and developers.
Centerpiece
Reported story with a named operator and real controlled audio, if permission exists.
Package
Written story, audio proof, short video, operator podcast, customer LinkedIn post, and developer noise fixture.
Distribution
Restaurant and franchise podcasts, Deepgram Learn, LinkedIn, developer communities, and partner channels.
Success
Qualified restaurant conversations, customer proof, and reusable developer examples.

Dealbreaker: Deepgram’s public materials do not name a restaurant customer. Confirm access before naming one.

04 · Deeper editorial territory

What makes a voice sound human?

Pair a public voice test with an abstract essay about tone, timing, interruption handling, emotional context, and consistency. Use the test to show how Deepgram identifies, measures, and reproduces those qualities.

Written

Essay and benchmark methodology, with an explicit statement that the Audio Turing Test is a Deepgram editorial test, not an established scientific standard.

Audio and video

Examples that isolate one quality at a time, followed by a short engineer and editor discussion of what listeners heard.

Builder

Test fixture, scoring method, sample files, and instructions that let developers reproduce the evaluation.

Recurring

Quarterly scorecard that can publish weak results as well as strong results. If Deepgram cannot publish a failing quarter, this franchise should not launch.

05 · Audience and distribution

Different audiences need different surfaces.

The site explains Flux first. External distribution then sends each audience to the version that answers its question.

Enterprise buyers

Can I trust this in a real workflow?

Business article, customer proof, audio evidence, Scott’s point of view, enterprise podcast, and a conversation request.

Builders

Can I test and build with it?

Documentation, playground, benchmark, code, GitHub fixture, technical podcast, and direct discussion on Hacker News, Reddit, X, and partner communities.

Press

What is the larger story?

Human consequence, independent enterprise proof, customer access, and a demonstration that can be understood without trusting a model card.

General audience

Why should I care?

Short audio and video examples that make the problem understandable before introducing technical language.

Hacker News and Reddit

Give builders something to inspect.

Post the benchmark, fixture, limitations, and results. Invite criticism. Do not paste marketing copy or astroturf discussion.

X and LinkedIn

Give each speaker a distinct job.

Scott carries the point of view. Engineers explain the mechanism. The company account links to the canonical article and proof.

Podcasts and newsletters

Let people hear the difference.

Pitch an interruption demonstration, an enterprise workflow discussion, or the method behind the human voice scorecard.

Partners

Show the product in context.

Coordinate with Daily, LiveKit, Pipecat, jambonz, and Twilio only when the partner approves the claims and format.

06 · Editorial calendar

Early wins first. Franchise second.

Hannah did not ask for a complete editorial system in 90 days. She asked for one or two strong early wins and a credible framework for the next 6 to 12 months.

First 30 daysFind the proof

Confirm the public audience, customer access, partner permissions, executive bench, and the strongest interruption demonstration.

By 60 daysShip one story

Publish the interruption article and its audio and video package. Test business and builder distribution separately.

By 90 daysShip the second

Publish the restaurant or enterprise proof story, depending on confirmed access, and present the 6 to 12 month calendar.

Six to 12 monthsBuild the franchise

Run the voice scorecard, customer stories, builder benchmarks, executive essays, and quarterly editorial packages.

What still needs an internal answer

Which customer stories can be named? Which executives, engineers, and partners are available? Who was pitched already? What was the pre-launch win condition? Which claims, demos, and audio examples can go public?

What I would not promise

I would not promise Wall Street Journal coverage, name a warm media relationship that is not verified, call the launch a failure, or use paid video views as evidence of organic interest.

What the role would own

The editorial role would own story mining, reporting, audience adaptation, production quality, executive and engineer coaching, the calendar, and the learning loop from each release.
