01 / What Deepgram launched

The product is strong. The story is waiting.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

What the launch established

Deepgram shipped a technically meaningful model, explained its conversation-native design, and gave developers a reason to try it. Scott’s interview adds the scale: more than 2,000 products built on Deepgram and more than 200,000 developers using it.

What the launch left open

The broader question: why does interruption handling matter to the people who have to trust, buy, deploy, or live with voice agents? The interview points to the next layer: a real-time voice system working beside slower reasoning and customer data.

03 / What I would do over 90 days

Turn one launch into a conversation.

Start with the Flux recovery story. Use events, product releases, customer access, and online discussion to decide where Deepgram can add evidence instead of noise.

Days 1 to 30 / Find the opening

Record and report the interruption test.

Interview a Flux product expert, review the launch and current voice-agent discussions, write the question “Can the agent recover after a correction?”, then record the same script twice: one clean recovery and one repeated response.

DeliverA reviewed 60-second audio comparison, story brief, and public test.MeasureAudio starts, full listens, test starts, and evaluation requests.StopIf both results and the method cannot be disclosed, keep the package internal.
Days 31 to 60 / Enter the conversation

Publish the lead package.

Publish the reported feature, short video, executive brief, and public test. Put buyer versions on LinkedIn and enterprise newsletters. Put builder versions in documentation, the playground, Hacker News, Reddit, X, and GitHub. Pitch the human consequence to podcasts and press.

DeliverOne package with the same facts and different next actions.MeasureAudio completion, evaluation requests, documentation visits, playground starts, and completed tests.StopRework a format that earns attention but no usable audience action.
Days 61 to 90 / Earn the next proof

Report a real deployment decision.

Interview a named customer or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow, what changed, and what happened next. If no one can be named, publish a source-backed enterprise explainer and keep the customer story open.

DeliverEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer guide, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise requests, target-account demo requests, and completed buyer paths.StopDelay the story if there is no named source, decision, and result.
After day 90 / Build what proves itself

Turn the winning test into a repeatable editorial program.

Repeat only if the first package produces repeat builder use or qualified buyer interest. Months 4 to 6: publish one proof-led story each month and improve the test from reader questions. Months 7 to 12: publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, and code as a recurring benchmark. Show gains and weak results.
