AI Gets Interrupted. Can It Keep the Thread?

A Flux launch audit, three stories already inside the product, and a 90-day plan for putting Deepgram into the conversations it wants to own.

01 / What Deepgram launched

The product is strong. The story is waiting.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

What the launch established

Deepgram shipped Flux as a conversation-native model for real-time voice agents, not just a prettier text-to-speech voice. The wider launch package makes the product, runtime, developer path, and scale legible.

What the launch left open

The package still does not show the human proof: when a caller changes the facts mid-sentence, can the agent update its answer without losing the thread, repeating itself, or making the caller start over? That is the opening for a story, a buyer proof point, and a test a builder can run.

04 / Story pitch

Customer cuts in. Can Flux keep up?

Build with a Deepgram developer. Hear what Flux does when a customer cuts in.

The use case

A drive-through order that changes mid-conversation.

Why this storyCan the agent update the order?

A noisy drive-through makes the problem ordinary, immediate, and easy to understand.

The story

A customer orders at a rough drive-through speaker, then changes the order: two meals, no onions, pay at the window. The customer is already late. The story follows the correction and what the agent does next.

Build with developers

Make the failure hearable.

Work with a Deepgram developer to create the live interaction, then record the same exchange through a representative baseline.

Publish the comparison

Give every audience a reason to care.

Press gets the customer frustration. Buyers get a concrete workflow. Builders get a test they can run.

05 / What I would do over 90 days

Turn one launch into a conversation.

Start with Flux. Let events, releases, customer access, and live voice-AI conversations decide what we make next.

Week 1 / Define the story

Choose the interruption test.

Confirm the drive-through scenario with a product expert, set the reporting question, and agree on the access and developer support needed to make the comparison real.

DeliverA story brief, script, developer handoff, and recording plan.MeasureAccess confirmed, behavior testable, and method publishable.StopIf the behavior or method cannot be disclosed, keep the package internal.
Days 8 to 30 / Build and publish

Turn the test into the lead package.

Build the comparison with developers, record the matched audio, and publish the reported story, transcript, short video, and builder test. Use events, product releases, and current voice-agent discussion to place it where each audience already pays attention.

DeliverOne package with the same facts and a clear next action for each audience.MeasureAudio completions, evaluation requests, documentation visits, playground starts, and completed tests.StopRevise a format that earns attention but no usable audience action.
Days 31 to 60 / Find enterprise evidence

Report a real deployment decision.

Interview a named customer, operator, or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow, what changed, and what happened next. If access is unavailable, use public evidence about reliability, deployment, privacy, or operational risk without claiming a customer story.

DeliverEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer brief, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise requests, target-account conversations, and completed buyer paths.StopKeep the customer story open if there is no named source, permission, decision, and result.
Days 61 to 90 / Decide what holds

Repeat, revise, stop, or codify the method.

Repeat the package if it produces audience action and usable evidence. Revise the format if one moves without the other. Stop if neither moves. Codify the test only if Deepgram can publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, code, and weak results, then rerun it consistently across tone, timing, interruption handling, emotional context, and consistency.
