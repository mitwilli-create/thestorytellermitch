Make voice matter.

A Flux launch audit, three stories already inside the product, and a 90-day plan for putting Deepgram into the conversations it wants to own.

01 / What Deepgram launched

The product is strong. The story is waiting.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

What the launch established

Deepgram shipped a technically meaningful model, explained its conversation-native design, and gave developers a reason to try it.

What the launch left open

The broader question: why does interruption handling matter to the people who have to trust, buy, deploy, or live with voice agents?

Three stories already inside the launch

These are not three unrelated campaigns. They are three ways to make the same product relevant to different people.

01 / The human moment

When the caller cuts in

Can a voice agent recover before the person on the other end decides it cannot be trusted?

03 / What I would do over 90 days

Turn one launch into a conversation.

Start with the Flux recovery story. Use product moments, customer access, and online discussion to decide where Deepgram can add evidence instead of noise.

Days 1 to 30 / Find the opening

Report the interruption problem.

Use the Flux launch, voice-agent discussions, and the available product experts to refine one reported question. Record matched recovery and baseline audio with the same script and settings.

ShipA reviewed audio comparison, story brief, and public test.MeasureAudio completions, test starts, and qualified evaluation requests.StopIf both results and the method cannot be disclosed, keep the package internal.
Days 31 to 60 / Enter the conversation

Publish the lead package.

Turn the proof into a reported feature, short video, executive brief, and builder test. Place each version where that audience already discusses products: LinkedIn and enterprise newsletters for buyers; documentation, playground, Hacker News, Reddit, X, and GitHub for builders; podcasts and press for broader reach.

ShipOne package with the same facts and different next actions.MeasureAudio plays, evaluation requests, documentation visits, playground starts, and completed tests.StopRework a format that earns attention but no usable audience action.
Days 61 to 90 / Earn the next proof

Report a real deployment decision.

Interview a named customer or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow, what changed, and what happened next. If no one can be named, publish a source-backed enterprise explainer and keep the customer story open.

ShipEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer guide, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise conversations, target-account demo requests, and buyer-path completion.StopDelay the story if there is no named source, decision, and result.
After day 90 / Build what proves itself

Turn the winning test into a repeatable editorial program.

Repeat only if the first package produces repeat builder use or qualified buyer interest. Months 4 to 6: publish one proof-led story each month and improve the test from reader questions. Months 7 to 12: publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, and code as a recurring benchmark. Show gains and weak results.
