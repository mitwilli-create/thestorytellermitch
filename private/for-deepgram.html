01 / The Flux launch audit

The launch had the story.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

One question. One piece of evidence. Same facts. A different next step for each audience.

03 / The first 90 days

How I would run it.

Start with the Flux recovery story. Measure who uses the proof. Repeat only what moves a buyer or builder to act.

Days 1 to 30 / Make the proof

Record the interruption test.

Use one script, matched settings, and two runs: clean recovery and a baseline that repeats the completed sentence. Review both before publishing.

ShipA reviewed 60 to 90 second comparison and a public method.MeasureSet baselines for audio completions, qualified evaluation starts, and builder actions.StopIf we cannot show both runs and explain the setup, keep it internal.
Days 31 to 60 / Publish the proof

Ship the interruption story.

Turn the comparison into a reported feature, short video, executive brief, and builder fixture. Send business and builder paths separately.

ShipOne package with the same facts across formats.MeasureAudio completions, evaluation starts, documentation visits, playground starts, and fixture completions.StopRework or pause a format that produces no usable audience action.
Days 61 to 90 / Report a decision

Earn the enterprise story.

Interview a named customer or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow and what happened next. If no one can be named, publish a source-backed enterprise explainer instead.

ShipEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer path, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise conversations, target-account demo requests, and buyer-path completion.StopDelay the story if there is no named source, decision, or result.
After day 90 / Repeat what worked

Turn the test into a shared method.

Repeat this process only if the first package produces repeat builder use and qualified buyer interest. Publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, and code. Rerun it across tone, timing, interruption handling, emotional context, and consistency. If the method cannot be rerun or weak results cannot be published, revise or stop.

How I decide what to repeat

Before launch, set a baseline, target, analytics source, and review owner. Review the result on days 30, 60, and 90. Choose one: repeat, revise, or stop.

Who owns what

Editorial chooses the story, reports it, edits it, and owns the ship date. Product and Engineering verify technical claims. Developer Relations owns the docs and fixture. Growth and Sales define and track the buyer action. Communications decides whether the evidence clears the press bar.

Before publish
