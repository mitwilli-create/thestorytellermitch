Make voice matter.

A Flux launch audit, three stories already inside the product, and a 90-day plan for putting Deepgram into the conversations it wants to own.

01 / What Deepgram launched

The product is strong. The story is waiting.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller interrupts, Flux can help the agent continue from what the caller heard instead of repeating the completed part.

What the launch established

Deepgram shipped a technically meaningful model, explained its conversation-native design, and gave developers a reason to try it.

What the launch left open

The broader question: why does interruption handling matter to the people who have to trust, buy, deploy, or live with voice agents?

Three stories already inside the launch

These are not three unrelated campaigns. They are three ways to make the same product relevant to different people.

01 / The human moment

When the caller cuts in

Can a voice agent recover before the person on the other end decides it cannot be trusted?

Who it reachesGeneral readers and press hear the human consequence. Builders can run the test. Business leaders can evaluate the experience.
02 / The buying question

What makes a buyer say yes?

What does an enterprise team need to see before putting a voice agent into a real workflow?

Who it reachesBusiness leaders get deployment proof. Builders get implementation detail. The story comes from a named customer, partner, or public record.
03 / The use case

Can a restaurant hear every order?

What happens when drive-through noise, interruptions, prices, and order changes meet a voice agent?

Who it reachesRestaurant operators see the use case. Builders can run a noise test. Press gets a customer consequence, if access is real.

02 / What I would change

Lead with the moment. Then make it travel.

The launch had the product and the proof points. I would change the editorial order, the audience handoff, and the reader action.

The foil

What Deepgram shipped.

What I would have made.

The launch led with product and model details.

I would lead with the interruption, prove it with audio, and route each audience to a next action.

They shipped
  • Technical explanation.
  • Founder point of view.
  • Developer invitation to try Flux.
I would add
  • A human opening question.
  • A controlled before-and-after audio proof.
  • Separate buyer, builder, and press routes.

02 / Prove itLet people hear the difference.

03 / Route itGive each audience a next action.

Evaluate, run the test, or cover the consequence.

The proof point

Can the agent recover when the caller cuts in?

This is the story I would pitch: a voice agent earns trust when it keeps the correction and drops the sentence the caller already heard.

Choose a beat to hear the proof.

The first three clips establish the conversation. The fourth is the failure case.

What it adds: one click gives a buyer an immediate trust test, a builder a reproducible example, and a reporter a clear story.

03 / What I would do over 90 days

Turn one launch into a conversation.

Start with the Flux recovery story. Use product moments, customer access, and online discussion to decide where Deepgram can add evidence instead of noise.

Days 1 to 30 / Find the opening

Record and report the interruption test.

Interview a Flux product expert, review the launch and current voice-agent discussions, write the question “Can the agent recover after a correction?”, then record the same script twice: one clean recovery and one repeated response.

DeliverA reviewed 60-second audio comparison, story brief, and public test.MeasureAudio starts, full listens, test starts, and evaluation requests.StopIf both results and the method cannot be disclosed, keep the package internal.
Days 31 to 60 / Enter the conversation

Publish the lead package.

Publish the reported feature, short video, executive brief, and public test. Put buyer versions on LinkedIn and enterprise newsletters. Put builder versions in documentation, the playground, Hacker News, Reddit, X, and GitHub. Pitch the human consequence to podcasts and press.

DeliverOne package with the same facts and different next actions.MeasureAudio completion, evaluation requests, documentation visits, playground starts, and completed tests.StopRework a format that earns attention but no usable audience action.
Days 61 to 90 / Earn the next proof

Report a real deployment decision.

Interview a named customer or partner who can explain why voice entered a workflow, what changed, and what happened next. If no one can be named, publish a source-backed enterprise explainer and keep the customer story open.

DeliverEnterprise feature, deployment diagram, buyer guide, and executive adaptation.MeasureQualified enterprise requests, target-account demo requests, and completed buyer paths.StopDelay the story if there is no named source, decision, and result.
After day 90 / Build what proves itself

Turn the winning test into a repeatable editorial program.

Repeat only if the first package produces repeat builder use or qualified buyer interest. Months 4 to 6: publish one proof-led story each month and improve the test from reader questions. Months 7 to 12: publish the samples, scoring method, limitations, and code as a recurring benchmark. Show gains and weak results.
