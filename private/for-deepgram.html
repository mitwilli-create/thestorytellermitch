How I would build on Flux.

The Flux launch already had the foundation of a strong enterprise story: a problem callers recognize, a product mechanism that can be heard, and a founder explanation that drew attention. I would make that story the center of the package, then adapt it for builders, press, and a broader audience.

01 · Start with the caller

Flux helps a voice agent recover when someone interrupts.

Flux is Deepgram’s text to speech model for live voice agents. When a caller cuts in, Flux reports what the caller heard and what the model did not finish, so the agent can continue from the right place instead of repeating itself.

The human problem

A caller interrupts to correct an account number. Does the agent adjust, or does it repeat a sentence the caller already heard?

01 · ExplainStart with the moment

Open with the interruption before naming the mechanism.

02 · ShowPlay the proof

Run the same interrupted exchange through Flux and an approved baseline.

03 · ChoosePick a path

Send enterprise readers to operational proof and builders to the test method.

04 · ActMake it easy

Offer an evaluation path for buyers and a reproducible test for builders.

02 · Build on what worked

What the launch established. What I would add.

The launch shipped the product, established company scale, and drew strong attention through Scott’s LinkedIn voice. I would keep those wins and give the human problem a larger role across the package.

What the launch established
  • Flux was available and ready to test.
  • The article opened with a caller experiencing a recognizable failure.
  • Scott’s LinkedIn post named interruptions, changed orders, and fast strings of numbers.
  • The founder interview supplied a useful contrast between conversation and audiobook performance.
  • The package served both enterprise and technical readers.
What I would add
  • Explain the interruption mechanism before separating the business and developer paths.
  • Use controlled audio as the main proof.
  • Make enterprise buyers the editorial tiebreaker.
  • Give builders the method, fixture, limitations, and code.
  • Set the reader action and measure before production begins.

03 · Three story packages

One central story, produced for several formats.

Each package begins with a reporting question. Written, audio, video, social, and developer formats help different readers examine the same story.

01 · Lead enterprise package

The half second after a caller interrupts

A reported story about interruption, memory, and the moment a caller decides whether to trust a voice agent.

Audience
Enterprise buyers first, followed by builders and press.
Centerpiece
Deepgram Learn story with a controlled audio comparison.
Package
Before and after audio, a 60 to 90 second video, a Scott LinkedIn essay, an engineer explanation, a podcast segment, and a public test fixture.
Distribution
LinkedIn and enterprise newsletters first. Hacker News, Reddit, X, GitHub, documentation, partner communities, podcasts, and press receive audience-specific versions.
Measure
Engaged reading, audio completion, technical evaluation clicks, developer test use, and relevant discussion.

Dependency: use the same script, settings, and disclosed method in the comparison. Do not attach a customer outcome without a named source.

02 · Enterprise trust package

What enterprises need before they trust a voice agent

Use IBM’s public statement as partner validation, not independent proof and not evidence that IBM is a Flux customer.

Audience
Enterprise buyers, business readers, and partner teams.
Centerpiece
Reported story about reliability, deployment, privacy, and operational trust.
Package
Written feature, deployment diagram, short video, executive adaptation, podcast discussion, and a concise buyer guide.
Distribution
Deepgram Learn and enterprise newsletters. IBM and partner channels only with approval. Communications can decide whether the reporting supports a press pitch.
Measure
Engaged enterprise readers, technical evaluation clicks, partner use, and content use in qualified sales work.

Dependency: the public IBM statement is enough for a first version. Interviews, joint claims, and channel use require IBM approval.

03 · Conditional customer package

Can a voice agent hear an order over engine noise?

A reported drive-through story about noise, interruptions, food names, prices, order changes, and human handoffs.

Audience
Restaurant operators, enterprise buyers, customer experience leaders, and builders.
Centerpiece
A named operator story with controlled audio if customer permission exists.
Package
Written feature, audio proof, short video, operator interview, customer social version, and a developer noise fixture.
Distribution
Restaurant and franchise newsletters and podcasts, Deepgram Learn, LinkedIn, developer communities, and approved partner channels.
Measure
Engaged operator readership, audio completion, technical evaluation clicks, and reuse of the fixture.

Dependency: no public source names a restaurant customer. If access is unavailable, report the acoustics problem without implying a customer deployment.

04 · A longer editorial franchise

A human voice benchmark that can publish losses.

Scott has publicly committed Deepgram to passing an Audio Turing Test at scale in 2026. Editorial could turn that commitment into a transparent benchmark with a fixed method, repeatable tests, and results that can fail.

Written

Method. Define tone, timing, interruption handling, emotional context, and consistency. State clearly that this is a Deepgram editorial benchmark, not an established scientific standard.

Audio and video

Listening test. Isolate one quality at a time, then have an engineer and editor explain what listeners heard.

Builder

Reproduction kit. Publish the prompts, sample files, scoring method, limitations, and code needed to repeat the test.

Recurring

Quarterly scorecard. Use the same method each quarter and publish weaknesses as well as gains. Secure leadership agreement on that standard before launch.

05 · Audience and distribution

One enterprise story, four audience versions.

Enterprise buyers set the editorial priority. Builders, press, and general readers receive versions that answer their own questions without changing the underlying facts.

Enterprise buyers

Can I trust this in a live workflow?

Use reported operational proof, controlled audio, deployment constraints, and a clear technical evaluation path.

Builders

Can I test the claim?

Publish documentation, code, GitHub fixtures, limitations, and direct discussion on Hacker News, Reddit, X, and partner communities.

Press

What is the consequence beyond the release?

Offer a human scene, named sources, disclosed methods, and evidence that can be examined without taking Deepgram’s claims on trust.

General audience

What changes for the person on the call?

Use short audio and video examples before introducing product language.

Hacker News and Reddit

Give builders something to inspect.

Publish the fixture, method, limitations, and complete results. Join the discussion as a source, not as a marketing account.

X and LinkedIn

Give each speaker a distinct job.

Scott carries the point of view. Engineers explain the mechanism. The company account points readers to the full story and evidence.

Podcasts and newsletters

Let people hear the difference.

Offer a demonstration listeners can hear, an enterprise deployment question, or the method behind the benchmark. Do not imply an existing relationship.

Partner communities

Show the product in context.

Adapt the technical package for Daily, LiveKit, Pipecat, jambonz, and Twilio only after each partner approves its claims and participation.

06 · First 90 days

Ship one or two wins, then establish the framework.

The first 90 days should produce one or two finished packages and a practical editorial framework for the next 6 to 12 months.

First 30 daysSet the reporting conditions

Confirm customer access, partner permissions, available experts, approved demonstrations, the enterprise reader action, and the measure for each package.

By 60 daysShip the interruption package

Publish the story, controlled audio, short video, executive adaptation, and builder fixture. Distribute the enterprise and builder versions separately.

By 90 daysShip the enterprise trust package

Use public evidence if partner interviews are delayed. Begin the restaurant package only if named access is confirmed.

Six to 12 monthsBuild a repeatable system

Commission customer reporting, builder benchmarks, founder and engineer essays, and a quarterly human voice scorecard.

Decisions needed before production

Which customers can be named? Which executives, engineers, and partners are available? What has already been pitched? Which demonstrations and audio can be published? What reader action and measure should be set before work begins?

Claims I would not assume

I would not imply customer access, partner approval, or a media relationship that has not been confirmed. I would disclose comparison methods, identify Deepgram-run benchmarks, and separate paid distribution from organic response.

What editorial would own

Editorial would own story mining, reporting, commissioning, editing, audience versions, production standards, the calendar, and the review of what each package taught us. Communications, Developer Relations, Growth, and Sales would remain partners in distribution and conversion.
