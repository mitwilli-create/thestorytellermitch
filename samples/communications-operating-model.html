The communications operating model

Safely redacted retained originals. Mitchell Williams, owner of the Strategic Communications Framework, updated February 3, 2026. Selected framework text and a separately labeled excerpt of the 2025 Multi-Channel Communications Strategy. Brackets replace internal names; tables are reformatted. Service targets describe the model, not measured delivery results.

Three levels of support

All communications requests enter through a single intake point: [communications intake]. Each submission is automatically triaged into one of three service tiers based on scope, audience, urgency, strategic alignment, and cost of failure.

Tier 1: Low Touch

Definition: Quick turnaround optimization for routine communications: email drafts, team announcements, newsletter contributions, standard templates.

Deliverables: Revised draft; rationale explaining every edit (why each change was made).

Requester responsibilities: Submit complete draft via intake form. Review changes and accept/reject based on context. Own final accuracy and approval workflows.

Timing: Same-day for simple revisions. The framework also sets a maximum of 48 hours during high volume.

Tier 2: Medium Touch

Definition: Strategic consultation and framework development for multi-phase initiatives: new program launches, mandate rollouts, cross-org partnerships.

Medium Touch requests receive a communications strategy playbook that includes target audiences, phased messaging, email templates, [frequently asked questions], and execution timeline. The [Communications] lead provides the architecture; the requesting program lead owns execution.

Deliverables: Communications strategy document; phased messaging templates (initiate, sustain, close); target audience analysis and channel recommendations; [frequently asked questions] and talking points as needed.

Requester responsibilities: Execute the strategy as designed. Own all approvals, accuracy checks, and timeline management. Escalate to Communications if strategy needs adjustment mid-flight.

Delivery target: 3-5 business days.

Tier 3: High Touch

Definition: End-to-end strategic partnership for high-stakes initiatives where the [Communications Lead] owns or co-owns execution: executive communications, external-facing content.

High Touch requests receive full Communications partnership from intake through execution and post-delivery analysis. This includes discovery conversations, stakeholder alignment, draft creation, approval coordination, distribution support, and success measurement.

Deliverables: Discovery session to clarify objectives and constraints; complete communications strategy and execution plan; drafted and refined materials (emails, slides, talking points); approval workflow coordination; distribution and follow-up support; post-project metrics and lessons learned.

Requester responsibilities: Provide complete context, background docs, and stakeholder map. Participate in discovery and strategy sessions. Facilitate access to subject matter experts and approvers. Own final content accuracy and technical precision.

Delivery target: 1-4 weeks depending on scope.

Decision logic: If the majority of factors indicate Low and there are NO High indicators, Low Touch. If 2+ factors indicate High, High Touch. Otherwise, Medium Touch.

Automatic High Touch triggers: Any request involving [the executive sponsor] (sender or primary audience); external-facing content and announcements; crisis or time-sensitive reputation management.

Choose the channel, then set the cadence

Separate retained source · Multi-Channel Communications Strategy, 2025

These phases represent a menu of communication options, not a required linear sequence.

  1. Intake & Registration: Identify and track communication opportunities.
  2. Internal Sync & Alignment: Align internal teams and close partners before broader announcements.
  3. Leadership Buy-In: Show the strategic value of the work.
  4. Technical Community: Select channels for the relevant technical audience.
  5. Broader Recognition & Impact: Prepare submissions for wider recognition.

Who does what

Communications lead: Consulting with teams to develop communication plans. Guiding teams in selecting the appropriate communication channels. [Providing] specific templates catered to each channel. Assisting teams in revising and pitching content for various channels. Tracking the success of communication efforts.

Requesting team: Providing the necessary information about the achievement. Collaborating to develop the communication plan. Drafting the initial content for review with guidance and templates. Presenting the achievement in the selected forums.

Leadership: Reviewing and approving communication plans and content for high-visibility announcements.

When requests compete: If multiple wins are in the pipeline for similar channels and timelines, the [Communications Lead] will confer with the [team] leads to determine which channel is best for each win.

Placement is not guaranteed. The final decision depends on the channel owners and the relevance of the win to their audience.

Source boundaries

The framework identifies Mitchell as its owner. The strategy assigns these responsibilities to the communications lead.
