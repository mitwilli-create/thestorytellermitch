Teddy Bear Holiday Party press material

A local event story shaped into a release for an organizer’s press outreach.

Safely redacted retained original · December 2025 kit · Written by Mitchell Williams and handed to the organizer. Selected historical release prose; private contacts and conflicting event details are omitted.

Seattle, WA , What began as a loud, joyful Christmas party among friends has grown into one of Seattle’s most beloved holiday traditions, one that now draws hundreds of attendees, including guests flying in from other states, and delivers hundreds of teddy bears and plush toys each year to children in need.

[Host identities and private home details omitted.]

Like many close-knit friend groups, guests would arrive bearing bottles of wine, small gifts, or thoughtful trinkets. After several years, [the hosts] realized they didn’t need anything, but their friends always wanted to bring something.

“Instead of gifts for us, we thought, why not give them something meaningful to give?” [the host] said.

About 11 years ago, the hosts made a simple request: bring one or more teddy bears instead.

[Beneficiary history omitted because retained versions disagree on the current beneficiary’s name.]

Once the party became a charity event, the community fully embraced it.

Today, the Teddy Bear Holiday Party draws 300 to 400 attendees, many of whom plan their holiday travel around the event. Guests fly in from across the country to attend what has become, for many, the unofficial start of the holiday season.

And while the mission is heartfelt, the party itself is intentionally anything but quiet.

[Private event logistics, named sponsors and year-specific donation details omitted.]

What has resonated most this year has been the response from guests themselves.

“We’ve received so many messages from people telling us that this party marks the beginning of the holidays for them,” [the host] said. “Hearing that, year after year, makes it clear this has become bigger than us.”

As the Teddy Bear Holiday Party enters its second decade, [the hosts] say the event now feels like it belongs to the community that built it.

“I don’t think I could ever stop doing this in good conscience,” [the host] said. “The generosity, the joy, and the impact are all far greater than we ever imagined. Creating memories while doing something for those less fortunate feels like what Christmas should be about.”

Sometimes, all it takes is asking people to bring a teddy bear, and throwing one unforgettable party.
