Leadership communications · Google

What I wrote.What I produced.Where the line is.

At Google, much of my thought-leadership work carried someone else's name. Sometimes I wrote the argument. Sometimes I produced the setting in which a leader delivered it. The distinction matters: ghostwriting, coaching, production, and operational communications are different jobs.

Ghostwriting · executive voice system Megan Kacholia · VP of Engineering

I wrote in her voice. Then I encoded how she made decisions.

The work covered quarterly organization remarks, rapid-response talking points, and signed emails after high-stakes moments. Then I turned the patterns behind her decisions into a production voice system: honesty markers, anti-spin rules, and separate spoken and written registers.

Complete redacted sample

Hi everyone,

I wanted to follow up after the [internal review meeting] this afternoon.

I (probably obviously) did not like how the meeting went, and when something like that happens I feel compelled to address it.

I’m sorry for being visibly frustrated in the discussion. I clearly wanted to have a different conversation about how we’re thinking about things versus the conversation we were set up to have, which unsurprisingly led to some tense interactions. I do not want folks to walk away with the impression that I don’t appreciate all the hard work that is going on (because that would be a very false impression. I’m aware of and greatly appreciate all the hard work the team is doing), but I also want to make sure we deal with some of the different points that were raised so we don’t repeat the same type of discussion again next quarter.

I do feel like there are some conversations that have been missing around how we are approaching or adjusting some of our [internal planning goals]; we need to make sure those conversations actually happen in our review forums rather than get “caught” in these [internal review process]. I have a few specific areas I’d like to have conversations on that I will request follow-ups for, and I encourage the leads to raise any areas they feel we may be missing conversations on.

We are a small team, but there’s so much we can deliver on across Google. I want us to be pushing ourselves to really think about what is best for [the organization], and hold ourselves to a high bar.

Let’s continue to all take on responsibility for improving our [internal planning] process, work together to adjust things when needed, and focus on delivering on our outcomes.

Best,
Megan

Executive ghostwriting · delivery coaching Ben Fried · CIO and VP, Corporate Engineering

Say what changed. Show the math. Make the call.

I wrote the weekly addresses and talking points, built the decks, and coached delivery. For a Japan committee address, I produced the English cut and Japanese-language version. One retained 2,142-word speech names five time-bound priorities, a quantified staffing gap, and the choices the organization had to make next.

Operational communications · behavior change Suzanne Timmons · Director, Corporate Engineering

Teach 75,000 new hires how Google works.

This was not a keynote. It was operational writing: turn unfamiliar internal systems into steps a new hire could complete without a specialist beside them. I wrote the Day-One orientation, credential-setup scripts, TechStop self-help videos, and recurring communications.

Complete redacted sample

Introduction, Suzanne Timmons:

I think we should open the piece with an animated screen of someone typing in [internal support URL] and then it transitioning to Suzanne on-camera in front of a TechStop. I’d love to suggest some type of watercolor transition to go in and out of the animations and video footage.

In this section, we need to establish what TechStop is and how it works very briefly before explaining what the service offers Googlers and how they should best use it.

Since we opened our first help desks in 2003, TechStop’s goal has always been the same: to keep your technology working for you, so you can stay productive. Today, we offer a suite of services to ensure that this happens, making support available where and when you need it.

The video should briefly touch on the suite of services that we will unpack further: [internal support URL], the online help center, self-service stations, grab-and-go loaners, accessory racks, storage lockers, [internal electronics store], and apps like [internal app].

TechStop anywhere: Every Googler can access remote services from anywhere and at any time through [internal support URL]. Visit the page to get tech support through chat, available 24 hours a day five days a week; phone, available 24 hours a day seven days a week; or a ticket for issues that do not require immediate attention. These services are staffed by the same techs who provide in-person support at our walk-up service desks.

The techs are real people from all over the world who care about helping you get the solutions you need in a quick and timely manner.

Appointment-based desk visits: At some offices where there’s no physical TechStop, we can schedule a desk visit when you have an issue requiring in-person support, and a tech will come to you. We look forward to putting in place a system that allows us to connect with you through [internal video platform] via a mobile app or your laptop so that the in-person interaction can happen from anywhere. Googlers are constantly on the go, more so than ever, and we want to deliver this support experience when you need it, wherever you need it.

Self-service stations: These stations give you easy access to hardware you may need and do not require the assistance of a tech. Grab-and-go loaners let you stay productive if you are having issues with your laptop or accidentally left it at home. Sign in to the temporary Chromebook and you are good to go.

Grab-and-go enables Googlers to stay productive by acting as a safety net with short-term loaners for unforeseen rainy days. The success of the program starts with each Googler being mindful of usage reasons and returning loaners in a timely manner. We prioritize primary device failure, primary device lost, trial Chromebook or Chrome OS, and temporary usage. Personal convenience, use as a permanent or secondary laptop, habitual loans, team usage, prearranged activities, and holding a loaner longer than needed affect the service level and availability of loaners.

Accessory racks provide hardware accessories like cables, chargers, and adapters. Grab what you need when you need it. Return lockers let you return devices like laptops and phones that you are no longer using without handing them to a tech. Grab-and-go loaners need to be returned to the station where you got them.

Online store: The online stop for Google electronics, tech accessories, and information technology access includes computer hardware, accessories, mobile devices, and software.

Cloud section: At Google, we take a cloud-first approach. Most of the tools we use every day are online. We use our external products internally as well. Our ultimate goal is to be a model enterprise for our customers. That means we show our users what is possible with our products by trying them out first.

With that said, Chromebooks are the preferred platform at Google. We’ve found that almost 87% of job roles can be productive when working off Chrome OS. If you’ve never used a Chromebook, do not worry. We have resources available to get you up and running.

Helpful apps: We also offer apps to help you find your way here at Google, like [internal app]. The mobile application includes a map feature that helps Googlers find their next meeting or a nearby cafe, a search feature for people, places, or resources, and access to a teams page with contact details for any Googler.

Conclusion, Suzanne Timmons: As we look ahead to continued growth and change at Google, we envision a TechStop that makes support even more highly available, more tailored to your evolving needs, and more effective in its execution. We envision a TechStop that empowers you with self-help solutions so effortless and effective that you can bypass assistance altogether, a TechStop that doesn’t require you to stop at all.
